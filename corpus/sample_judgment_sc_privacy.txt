JUDGMENT

[1] The reference to the nine Judge Bench arises out of a challenge to the constitutional validity of the Aadhaar card scheme. The petitioners contend that the scheme infringes the fundamental right to privacy guaranteed by Part III of the Constitution.

[2] In M.P. Sharma v. Satish Chandra, District Magistrate, Delhi (1954) SCR 1077 and Kharak Singh v. State of U.P. (1964) 1 SCR 332, observations were made by benches of eight and six Judges respectively to the effect that the Constitution does not specifically protect a right to privacy.

[3] The question which falls for determination before this Bench is whether the right to privacy is a fundamental right guaranteed under Part III of the Constitution of India.

[4] Privacy is a constitutionally protected right which emerges, primarily, from the guarantee of life and personal liberty in Article 21 of the Constitution. Elements of privacy also arise in varying contexts from the other facets of freedom and dignity recognised and guaranteed by the fundamental rights contained in Part III.

RATIO DECIDENDI: The right to privacy is protected as an intrinsic part of the right to life and personal liberty under Article 21 and as a part of the freedoms guaranteed by Part III of the Constitution. The decisions in M.P. Sharma and Kharak Singh to the extent they hold that privacy is not a fundamental right stand overruled.
