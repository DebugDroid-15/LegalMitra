Can a High Court quash an FIR in a commercial dispute under Section 528 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023? What are the key Supreme Court principles?

Sec 528 BNSS (erstwhile Sec 482 CrPC) Law applicable as of: July 2024 – Present
verified Short Answer

Yes. Under Section 528 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023 (which retains and codifies the plenary inherent powers previously enshrined under Section 482 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973), High Courts retain absolute, plenary jurisdiction to quash an FIR, chargesheet, or pending criminal proceedings where the grievance arose out of an essentially commercial or contractual dispute, provided the criminal machinery has been set into motion primarily as an engine of oppression, arm-twisting, or recovery intimidation.

balance Statutory Foundation (Relevant Law)

Substantive Transition

Upon the commencement of the new criminal codes on July 1, 2024, Section 528 of the BNSS replicates the exact statutory architecture of Section 482 of the CrPC verbatim. The legislative intent intentionally preserved unbroken continuity with established Supreme Court jurisprudence regarding High Court inherent oversight:

Section 528 BNSS (Act 46 of 2023) — Verbatim Clause Pari Materia Sec 482 CrPC
“Nothing in this Sanhita shall be deemed to limit or affect the inherent powers of the High Court to make such orders as may be necessary to give effect to any order under this Sanhita, or to prevent abuse of the process of any court or otherwise to secure the ends of justice.”

gavel Supreme Court Guiding Principles & Test of Civil vs. Criminal

The Supreme Court of India has consistently held that criminal courts cannot be deployed as debt collection agencies or substitutes for recovery suits under the Specific Relief Act or Indian Contract Act, 1872. Three primary doctrinal tests govern quashing:

1

Absence of Fraudulent / Dishonest Intent at Inception

Bhajan Lal Doctrine

Mere failure to hold up a commercial promise does not constitute an offence of cheating under Section 318 BNS (erstwhile Sec 420 IPC). The Apex Court has clarified that the complainant must substantiate an explicit dishonest intention at the very threshold when the contract was entered into. If mens rea at inception is missing, the remedy resides squarely in damages or specific performance.

2

Breach of Contract vs. Criminal Breach of Trust

Paramjeet Batra Rule

A sharp, impermeable demarcation exists between a civil breach of an agreement and the offence of Criminal Breach of Trust under Section 316 BNS (erstwhile Sec 405/406 IPC). Unless there is evidence of dominion over property followed by intentional misappropriation or conversion for personal use, the High Court is bound under Section 528 to quash the proceeding.

3

Abuse of Process & Camouflaged Criminal Prosecution

Usha Chakraborty Test

Where the High Court discovers that an FIR has been lodged solely to force a commercial settlement, compel forfeiture of arbitration clauses, or inflict pre-trial incarceration on corporate directors, exercise of Section 528 is not merely discretionary—it is a judicial obligation to prevent court proceedings from morphing into instruments of harassment.

shield_with_heart Exceptions & Limitations (When High Courts Will Decline Quashing)

High Courts exercise extraordinary restraint under Section 528 and will NOT interfere under the following conditions:

  • remove Presence of Independent Criminal Mens Rea: When allegations reveal fabricated records, forged invoices, or parallel criminal conspiracy along with breach of contract.
  • remove Heavily Disputed Questions of Fact: High Courts will not conduct a mini-trial at the Section 528 stage to evaluate disputed balances, unsigned delivery receipts, or WhatsApp chats.
  • remove Dual Liability Permissibility: If an act constitutes both a civil wrong and a recognizable criminal offence simultaneously, mere civil pendency is not an absolute bar to trial.
