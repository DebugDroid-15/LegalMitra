## 1. Historical Evolution of Indian Patent Law

The Patents Act, 1970 (Act No. 39 of 1970) repealed the Indian Patents and Designs Act, 1911 following recommendations of the Ayyangar Committee Report (1959). It sought to foster self-reliant domestic pharmaceutical manufacturing.

### Three TRIPS Phased Amendment Acts
1. **Act 17 of 1999 (Patents (Amendment) Act, 1999)**: W.r.e.f. 01.01.1995. Introduced the "mailbox rule" under Section 5(2) and Exclusive Marketing Rights (EMRs) under Chapter IVA.
2. **Act 38 of 2002 (Patents (Amendment) Act, 2002)**: Commenced 20.05.2003. Extended patent term to uniform 20 years, inverted burden of proof for process patents, and codified compulsory licensing provisions.
3. **Act 15 of 2005 (Patents (Amendment) Act, 2005)**: Commenced 01.01.2005 (replacing Ordinance 7 of 2004). Landmark TRIPS compliance reform introducing product patents for pharmaceuticals and Section 3(d) safeguards.

## 2. Section 3(d) Temporal Transition

### 2.1 Pre-2005 Regime (`[1972-04-20, 2005-01-01)`)
> *"3(d) the mere discovery of any new property or new use for a known substance or of the mere use of a known process, machine or apparatus unless such known process results in a new product or employs at least one new reactant."*

Combined with Section 5 (which forbade product claims for medicines, allowing only process patents), product claims on pharmaceuticals were legally barred.

### 2.2 Post-2005 Regime (`[2005-01-01, 9999-12-31)`)
> *"3(d) the mere discovery of a new form of a known substance which does not result in the enhancement of the known efficacy of that substance or the mere discovery of any new property or new use for a known substance or of the mere use of a known process, machine or apparatus unless such known process results in a new product or employs at least one new reactant.*  
> *Explanation.—For the purposes of this clause, salts, esters, ethers, polymorphs, metabolites, pure form, particle size, isomers, mixtures of isomers, complexes, combinations and other derivatives of known substance shall be considered to be the same substance, unless they differ significantly in properties with regard to efficacy."*

## 3. Doctrinal Interpretation: Novartis AG v. Union of India (2013) 6 SCC 1

The Supreme Court conclusively resolved the interpretation of "efficacy" under Section 3(d):
1. **Therapeutic Efficacy**: In the context of pharmaceuticals, "efficacy" means therapeutic efficacy—the ability of the drug to cure, alleviate, or prevent disease.
2. **Physicochemical Properties Irrelevant**: Beneficial physical properties such as 30% higher bioavailability, improved flowability, thermodynamic stability, or lower hygroscopicity do not satisfy Section 3(d) unless they directly translate to demonstrable superiority in therapeutic efficacy.
3. **Evergreening Prevention**: Section 3(d) acts as a strict statutory filter preventing pharmaceutical companies from extending patent monopolies through minor variations.
