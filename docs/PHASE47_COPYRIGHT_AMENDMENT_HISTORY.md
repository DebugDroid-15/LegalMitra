## 1. Legislative Chronology of the Copyright Act, 1957

The Copyright Act, 1957 (Act No. 14 of 1957) was enacted on 4 June 1957 and commenced on 21 January 1958 (S.R.O. 269), replacing the colonial Indian Copyright Act, 1914.

### Key Amending Enactments
1. **Act 23 of 1983**: Enacted compulsory licensing for Indian works and translations in compliance with the Paris Act (1971) of the Berne Convention.
2. **Act 65 of 1984**: Introduced anti-piracy provisions and enhanced criminal penalties for cinematograph and audio piracy.
3. **Act 13 of 1992**: Extended term of copyright from 50 years to 60 years post mortem auctoris.
4. **Act 38 of 1994**: Comprehensive overhaul protecting computer programs as literary works, satellite broadcasting rights, and performer's rights.
5. **Act 49 of 1999**: Harmonized statutory provisions with the TRIPS Agreement.
6. **Act 27 of 2012 (The Copyright (Amendment) Act, 2012)**: The most transformative digital-era reform, enacted on 07.06.2012 and commenced on 21.06.2012 (S.O. 1393(E)).

## 2. Forensic Reconstruction of the 2012 Amendment

| Provision | Pre-2012 Statutory Formulation | Post-2012 Substituted Formulation | Legal Impact & Doctrinal Authority |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Section 57 (Moral Rights)** | Author had right to restrain distortion/mutilation *"before the expiration of the term of copyright"*. | Restriction removed; Section 57(2) explicitly allows legal representatives to enforce moral rights indefinitely. | Moral rights of paternity and integrity made **perpetual**; survives copyright expiration. |
| **Section 52(1)(a) (Fair Dealing)** | Limited strictly to "fair dealing with a literary, dramatic, musical or artistic work" for private use, research, criticism. | Expanded to "fair dealing with any work, not being a computer programme, for private or personal use". | Extended to all works; allows personal archiving. |
| **Section 52(1)(i) (Education)** | Reproduction by a teacher or pupil in the course of instruction. | Clarified and confirmed in judicial precedent (*Rameshwari Photocopy Service*). | Allows course packs compiled by universities for instructional purposes without license fee. |
| **Section 52(1)(zb) (Disability)** | No express statutory exception for accessible formats. | Complete non-infringement exception for conversion into accessible formats (Braille, audiobooks) for persons with disabilities. | Implements Marrakesh VIP Treaty obligations into domestic law. |
| **Section 18 & 19 (Royalties)** | Lyricists and composers routinely assigned all rights to film producers without ongoing remuneration. | Provisos inserted to s.18(1) and s.19(9)/(10): Authors cannot assign or waive equal share of royalties. | Inalienable, non-waivable statutory royalty right for underlying authors in commercial uses. |

## 3. Precedent Grounding
- *Eastern Book Company v. D.B. Modak* (2008) 1 SCC 1: Section 13 originality standard requires "modicum of creativity".
- *R.G. Anand v. Delux Films* (1978) 4 SCC 118: Idea-expression dichotomy under Section 51.
- *University of Oxford v. Rameshwari Photocopy Service* (2016) 160 DRJ 678: Section 52(1)(i) covers course pack preparation.
