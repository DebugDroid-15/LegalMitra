### 3. Structural Integrity and Verification Details

#### A. Companies Act 2013: Section 135 CSR Hierarchy
- Sub-section (1): Eligibility thresholds (Net worth $\ge$ 500 Cr, Turnover $\ge$ 1,000 Cr, or Net Profit $\ge$ 5 Cr).
- Sub-section (5): Mandate to spend at least 2% of average net profits of preceding three financial years.
  - *Proviso 1*: Preference to local areas where the company operates.
  - *Proviso 2*: Obligation to specify reasons if company fails to spend.
- Sub-section (6) *(inserted by Act 22 of 2019)*: Mandatory transfer of unspent ongoing project funds to an Unspent CSR Account within 30 days.
- Sub-section (7) *(substituted by Act 29 of 2020)*: Decriminalization of CSR defaults from penal fines to civil monetary penalties (twice the default amount or 1 Crore, whichever is less).

#### B. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016: Section 29A & 32A
- **Section 29A**: Contains 10 exhaustive disqualification clauses (a) through (j) with multiple provisos and explanations defining "connected persons".
- **Section 32A**: Two distinct limbs:
  1. *Sub-section (1)*: Immunity from liability for offences committed prior to CIRP once resolution plan is approved and control shifts to unrelated third parties.
  2. *Sub-section (2)*: Protection of debtor property from attachment, seizure, or confiscation by investigating agencies (PMLA / CBI) for prior offences (*Manish Kumar v. Union of India* (2021) 5 SCC 1).

#### C. Indian Contract Act 1872: Section 28 Restraint of Legal Proceedings
- Pre-1997: Barred only agreements absolutely restricting a party from enforcing contractual rights in ordinary tribunals.
- Post-1997: Added clause (b) rendering void any contract stipulation that extinguishes the rights of any party or discharges any party from liability on the expiry of a specified period (*Union of India v. Indusind Bank Ltd.* (2016) 9 SCC 720).

#### D. Transfer of Property Act 1882: Section 53A Part Performance
- Transformed from an equitable right under an unregistered agreement into a strictly conditioned statutory defense requiring a registered instrument under Section 17(1A) of the Registration Act, 1908.

#### E. CPC Order VIII Rule 1: Ordinary vs Commercial Suits
- In ordinary civil suits, the 30-day period extendable up to 90 days under the proviso to Order VIII Rule 1 is directory (*Kailash v. Nanhku* (2005) 4 SCC 480).
- In Commercial Suits governed by the Commercial Courts Act, 2015, the second proviso imposes a strict maximum of 120 days from summons service, after which the right to file the written statement is permanently forfeited (*SCG Contracts (India) Pvt. Ltd. v. K.S. Chamankar Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd.* (2019) 12 SCC 210).

#### F. Motor Vehicles Act 1988: Section 163A vs Section 164
- Section 163A (structured formula, subject to annual income cap of INR 40,000) was repealed and replaced by Section 164 effective 1 April 2022 (S.O. 859(E)), introducing no-fault fixed compensation of INR 5,00,000 in case of death and INR 2,50,000 for grievous hurt.
