### 3. Selection and Prioritization of Batch 2 Instruments

To balance high legal utility with rigorous temporal depth, Batch 2 acquires six pivotal Central Acts with rich amendment histories:

#### 1. Companies Act, 2013 (Act 18 of 2013)
- **Significance**: Foundation of Indian corporate regulation, director duties, corporate social responsibility, and corporate criminal liability.
- **Temporal Complexity**: Extensive amendments in 2015, 2017, 2019, and 2020. Specifically, Section 135 (CSR) evolved from a "comply or explain" disclosure regime into a mandatory unspent-fund escrow transfer system with statutory financial penalties.

#### 2. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (Act 31 of 2016)
- **Significance**: India's comprehensive corporate insolvency resolution process (CIRP) and liquidation framework.
- **Temporal Complexity**: Rapid legislative adjustments: Section 29A disqualification of promoters (Act 8 of 2018), homebuyer classification under Section 5(8)(f) (Act 26 of 2018), and Section 32A clean-slate immunity (Act 1 of 2020).

#### 3. Indian Contract Act, 1872 (Act 9 of 1872)
- **Significance**: Bedrock of all commercial and civil transactions in India.
- **Temporal Complexity**: The 1997 amendment to Section 28 (Act 1 of 1997) overturned decades of judicial precedents that upheld contract clauses extinguishing party rights upon expiry of stipulated notice periods.

#### 4. Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (Act 4 of 1882)
- **Significance**: Governing statute for immoveable property transfers, sales, mortgages, leases, and doctrine of part performance.
- **Temporal Complexity**: Registration and Other Related Laws (Amendment) Act, 2001 (Act 48 of 2001) amended Section 53A w.e.f. 24.09.2001, making contract registration mandatory to plead the equitable defense of part performance.

#### 5. Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (Act 5 of 1908)
- **Significance**: Procedural foundation of all civil litigation in Indian courts.
- **Temporal Complexity**: The Commercial Courts Act, 2015 (Act 4 of 2016) introduced a dual procedural track: ordinary civil suits permit discretionary written statement extensions beyond 90 days, while commercial disputes of Specified Value enforce a strict, non-extendable 120-day forfeiture under Order VIII Rule 1.

#### 6. Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (Act 59 of 1988)
- **Significance**: High-volume compensation litigation before Motor Accident Claims Tribunals (MACT).
- **Temporal Complexity**: Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019 (Act 32 of 2019) substituted Section 163A with Section 164 (payment of fixed compensation on no-fault basis: INR 5 Lakhs for death, INR 2.5 Lakhs for grievous hurt), brought into force w.e.f. 1 April 2022 via S.O. 859(E).
